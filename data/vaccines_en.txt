Vaccines train the immune system to recognise a germ without causing the disease. A vaccine contains a weakened or inactive part of the germ, or instructions for the body to make one harmless protein from it. After vaccination the body produces antibodies and memory cells. If the real germ appears later the immune system responds faster. Some vaccines need booster doses because protection fades over time. Common side effects are a sore arm and mild fever that last a day or two.
